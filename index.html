GUARDIANS OF THE PLANET

EXPLORE • LEARN • PROTECT • INSPIRE

Earth is our home, and protecting it is everyone's responsibility.

Become a Guardian by learning about our planet and making choices that help nature.

WHY EARTH MATTERS

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only planet currently known to support life.

Earth contains oceans, forests, mountains, deserts, rivers, wetlands, grasslands and polar regions.

These environments provide homes for millions of species.

Earth also provides humans with freshwater, food, materials, energy and places to live.

Plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide during photosynthesis to produce food and release oxygen.

Animals depend on plants and other organisms for food. This creates food chains and food webs.

Every ecosystem is connected. A change to one part of an ecosystem can affect many other organisms.

Protecting nature means protecting the systems that support life.

THE OVERFLOWING OCEAN

OUR BLUE PLANET

Oceans cover about 71 percent of Earth's surface.

The ocean is divided into different regions, including coastal waters, the open ocean and the deep ocean.

Oceans contain coral reefs, mangroves, seagrass meadows, kelp forests and deep-ocean ecosystems.

Ocean organisms range from tiny plankton to some of the largest animals on Earth.

Oceans absorb and store large amounts of heat, helping regulate Earth's climate.

Oceans also take part in the global carbon cycle.

Plastic pollution can enter the ocean through rivers, beaches and coastal areas.

Oil pollution, chemicals and other waste can also harm marine ecosystems.

Overfishing can reduce fish populations and change marine food webs.

Warmer ocean temperatures can place stress on marine organisms.

Coral reefs are especially sensitive to changes in ocean conditions.

Guardians can help by reducing waste, avoiding unnecessary plastic and keeping waterways clean.

MAJOR PLANET CHALLENGES

PLASTIC POLLUTION

Plastic is a useful material, but large amounts of plastic are used only once.

Plastic waste can travel through drains and rivers before reaching the ocean.

Some plastic can break into tiny pieces called microplastics.

Pollution can damage habitats and affect wildlife.

Guardians can reduce waste by reusing objects, recycling correctly and avoiding unnecessary single-use items.

CLIMATE CHANGE

Climate describes long-term patterns of temperature, rainfall and other conditions.

The greenhouse effect naturally helps keep Earth warm enough for life.

Human activities have increased the amount of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere.

Climate change can affect ecosystems, ice, oceans, weather patterns and sea levels.

Saving energy and supporting cleaner energy sources can help reduce emissions.

HABITAT LOSS

A habitat is the natural home of an organism.

Forests, wetlands, grasslands and oceans are important habitats.

When habitats are destroyed or changed, species can lose food, shelter and breeding areas.

Habitat loss is one of the major threats to biodiversity.

Protecting natural areas helps plants and animals survive.

EARTH FACTS

Earth is approximately 4.5 billion years old.

Earth is the third planet from the Sun.

About 71 percent of Earth's surface is covered by water.

Most of Earth's water is salt water found in oceans.

Only a small amount of Earth's water is easily available as freshwater for people and ecosystems.

Earth has one natural satellite, the Moon.

Earth's atmosphere contains nitrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide and other gases.

The atmosphere helps protect life and helps regulate Earth's temperature.

Earth has many different climates and ecosystems.

Scientists have identified millions of species, but many more may still be unknown.

The planet's land, oceans, atmosphere and living organisms interact as one connected Earth system.

THE POWER OF FORESTS

Forests cover large areas of land and provide habitats for countless organisms.

Trees use photosynthesis to take in carbon dioxide and produce oxygen and sugars.

Forests can store carbon in trees, roots and soil.

Forests also influence the movement of water through ecosystems.

Tree roots can help hold soil in place and reduce erosion.

Forests provide food and shelter for insects, birds, mammals, reptiles, fungi and microorganisms.

Tropical forests are especially rich in biodiversity.

Cutting down forests can destroy habitats and release stored carbon.

Protecting existing natural forests is an important part of protecting biodiversity.

OCEAN LIFE

The ocean contains an enormous variety of living organisms.

  • Coral reefs provide habitats for many marine species.
  • Mangroves provide shelter for young fish and protect coastlines.
  • Plankton are important parts of marine food webs.
  • Fish are important consumers in many ocean ecosystems.
  • Sea turtles depend on healthy marine habitats.
  • Whales are among the largest animals on Earth.
  • Deep-ocean organisms can survive in environments with little sunlight.

Marine food webs connect many different organisms. Changes in one population can affect other species.

Protecting marine habitats helps keep ocean ecosystems healthy.

CLIMATE CHANGE

Weather describes conditions over short periods, while climate describes long-term patterns.

Earth's natural greenhouse effect keeps the planet warmer than it would otherwise be.

Greenhouse gases include carbon dioxide, methane and water vapor.

Human activities have increased concentrations of several greenhouse gases.

  • Burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide.
  • Deforestation can reduce carbon storage.
  • Agriculture can produce greenhouse gases.
  • Warmer temperatures can affect ecosystems.
  • Melting land ice contributes to rising sea levels.
  • Oceans absorb much of the excess heat in the climate system.

Understanding climate science helps communities prepare for environmental changes.

BIODIVERSITY

Biodiversity means the variety of life on Earth.

It includes the variety of species, genes and ecosystems.

Biodiversity is important because organisms interact with one another in food webs and ecosystems.

Bees, butterflies and other pollinators help many plants reproduce.

Decomposers break down dead material and return nutrients to ecosystems.

Predators help control populations of other animals.

Plants provide food and habitats for many organisms.

Habitat destruction, pollution, invasive species, overuse of resources and climate change can threaten biodiversity.

Protecting habitats is one of the most important ways to protect biodiversity.

THE WATER CYCLE

Water is constantly moving through Earth's atmosphere, land and oceans.

Evaporation happens when liquid water becomes water vapor.

Plants release water vapor through a process called transpiration.

Condensation occurs when water vapor cools and forms tiny water droplets.

Precipitation occurs when water falls from clouds as rain, snow, sleet or hail.

Water can flow across land into rivers, lakes and oceans.

Some water enters the ground and becomes groundwater.

The water cycle connects ecosystems around the planet.

RENEWABLE ENERGY

Renewable energy comes from sources that are naturally replenished.

Solar energy uses energy from the Sun.

Wind energy uses moving air to generate electricity.

Hydroelectric power uses moving water.

Geothermal energy uses heat from inside Earth.

Renewable energy can help reduce dependence on fossil fuels.

Energy choices are important because producing and using energy can affect the environment.

POLLUTION

Pollution happens when harmful substances or forms of energy enter the environment.

Air pollution can affect human health, animals and plants.

Water pollution can damage rivers, lakes and oceans.

Soil pollution can affect plants and organisms living underground.

Noise pollution can interfere with animals that depend on sound.

Light pollution can affect animals that depend on natural day and night cycles.

Reducing pollution starts with understanding where it comes from and making better choices.

GUARDIAN TIPS

SAVE WATER

Turn off taps when water is not needed. Fix leaks and avoid wasting clean water.

SAVE ENERGY

Turn off lights and electronics when they are not being used.

REDUCE WASTE

Reuse, repair and recycle items whenever possible.

PROTECT WILDLIFE

Respect animals and never disturb their natural habitats.

KEEP NATURE CLEAN

Keep rubbish away from parks, beaches, rivers and oceans.

USE LESS PLASTIC

Choose reusable products when possible and avoid unnecessary single-use plastic.

GUARDIAN MISSION

A Guardian learns about the planet and uses that knowledge to make responsible decisions.

MISSION IDEAS

  • Start a recycling project at school.
  • Organize a local clean-up.
  • Teach others about ocean pollution.
  • Learn about local plants and animals.
  • Encourage people to save water.
  • Reduce unnecessary plastic use.
  • Plant and care for suitable native plants.
  • Share reliable environmental information.

THE GUARDIAN RULE

Before throwing something away, ask: Can I REDUCE it, REUSE it, REPAIR it or RECYCLE it?
